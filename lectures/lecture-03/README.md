# Lecture 03 - Command Line

Your first hour in the terminal: what the shell is, why it is worth learning, and the handful of commands you will use every day.

[View the slides](https://danilofreire.github.io/datasci350/lectures/lecture-03/03-command-line.html)

## What we cover

- What the kernel, the shell, and the terminal are, and how they differ
- Why the command line beats a mouse for speed, reproducibility, and remote work
- The Unix philosophy: small tools that each do one thing, combined to do big things
- Command syntax, `command [options] arguments`, and how flags like `-lah` combine
- Getting around with `pwd`, `ls`, and `cd`, plus the `~`, `.`, and `..` shortcuts
- Finding help with `man`, `tldr`, and `cht.sh`

## Practice

There are three exercises: a `man` page challenge to do in pairs, a navigation exercise we work through together, and a longer one to try at home. They all have you create, rename, and delete files from the terminal.

Be careful with `rm -r` and `rm -rf`. They delete immediately, with no Trash and no undo. Read the path before you press Enter.
